## 2. New findings, ranked

### N1. HIGH: the kill-switch flatten can fail completely and leave a full position open for hours [CONFIRMED]
*What happens*
* The flatten is one IOC at the touch ± 5 ticks, retried once per second, at most 5 times (`glft.py:605-628`, spec §7.1).
* Drawdown kills fire in fast moves. In a fast move, the touch the strategy sees (≈ 240 ms old) plus 132 ms of order latency is regularly more than 5 ticks stale.

*Evidence from the probe*
* **`higher_latency` OOS**, kill 16:46:59, q = −0.05:
  * all 5 IOC buys returned `canceled: no_liquidity`;
  * the ask ran 3540.31 → 3543.0 in 4 s;
  * the strategy then sat on a **naked −0.05 ETH for the remaining 7.2 h**, with no quoting and no further exits.
  * Max drawdown 5.61 is above the 5.0 limit. The final net of −3.46 looks *better* than base only because the price later fell.
* **`fee_shift` seed 4 OOS**: 4 of 5 attempts failed. The 5th filled.
* **Base seed 42 OOS**: 1 of 2 failed.
* So 3 of the 8 kills seen had at least one failure, and 1 of the 8 failed completely.

*Bias*
* Variants whose flatten fails get a free directional bet after the kill. That randomises their PnL, and it shows up in the sensitivity table as "better".
* On paper/live it is a real risk-control hole: the kill switch can leave a full position unmanaged.

*Fix*
* **quant-researcher** (spec §7.1):
  * escalate the price per attempt, e.g. slip = 5·2^(n−1) ticks, or a market order from the 2nd attempt (the engine supports `price=None` with tiered sweep);
  * keep retrying until flat, with a time cap rather than a count cap;
  * if still not flat, raise a loud `flatten_failed` state.
* **quant-developer**: implement it and add a test where the touch runs away for 5 timer ticks.
* **backtester**: report the position and time held after a kill, and the flatten cost.

### N2. HIGH (interpretation): variant comparisons are confounded by random latency paths and censored by kills [CONFIRMED]
*Cause*
* `engine.py:197` (order jitter) and `engine.py:334` (feed-delay draws) share **one** rng.
* Every action draws a jitter. A variant that acts differently therefore gets a different feed-delay path from its first differing action onwards.
* The variants are not common-random-number comparisons.

*Size*
* Seed noise of base net PnL: sd **0.36 IS / 0.32 OOS** over 5 seeds. For skew_off the IS sd is **1.05**.
* `order_jitter_ms: 0` alone moves base IS from −3.10 to −3.52.

*Censoring by kills*
* 7 of 22 seed-42 runs hit the 5 USDT drawdown kill:
  * base OOS;
  * pessimistic_fills OOS;
  * higher_latency IS and OOS;
  * latency_spikes IS;
  * pessimistic_all IS and OOS.
* So their PnL is cut off near −5, and their fill counts and "inventory PnL" cover different hours.
* **The base OOS kill is itself seed luck.** Seeds 1–4 do not kill (net −4.37 / −4.36 / −4.53 / −4.39, dd 4.70–5.01). Seed 42 is the worst case, −5.10.
* The table shows none of this.

*Fix (backtester)*
* Give feed delay, order jitter and the strategy separate child generators (`np.random.SeedSequence(seed).spawn(3)`).
* Add `killed` (cause and time), time at limit and mean \|q\| to `headline()` and the sensitivity table.
* For strategy-param variants (skew_off, fee_shift, γ), run ≥ 5 seeds and report mean ± sd and the paired sign count.

**H3 (skew controls inventory): HOLDS, not falsified.**
* The spec's test (§9) is: base has lower time at limit **and** lower mean \|q\| than skew_off. That is true in **10 of 10** seed-periods, by a wide margin: 2–4% vs 25–36% at the limit.
* skew_off's better PnL (better in 10 of 10 pairs) is a different effect. It is not a sign that "skew hurts":
  1. Pinned at ±Q for about 30% of the time, it quotes only one side then, so it fills about 18% less (655 vs 802). In a strategy that loses on every fill after fees (capture ≈ 30 ticks vs fee ≈ 71 ticks per ETH), fewer fills means smaller losses. Fees: −4.11 vs −5.21.
  2. Its inventory PnL is a large directional bet: +1.38 IS, −2.31 OOS, with sd about 1 across seeds.
* So the PnL ranking says "trade less", not "skew less". It must not be used to drop the skew. Doing so would also be OOS selection.

**H8 (exact fee folding beats the shift): HOLDS on its metric, not falsified.**
* `fee_shift` uses A rather than A·e^(−kc), so its ω is smaller at the same γ, which means a weaker skew.
* It has worse inventory control in 10 of 10 seed-periods: \|q\| 0.030 / 0.027 vs 0.018 / 0.016, and 24% / 16% vs 2% / 4% at the limit.
* Its better PnL (9 of 10 pairs; the exception is seed 4 OOS, where it was killed) has the same cause as skew_off: a weaker skew means less time two-sided and fewer fills.
* **Not evidence for the shift.**

**H6 (not an artefact of fills or latency): HOLDS.**
* No variant changes sign. All 52 runs are negative.
* No listed variant cuts fills by more than 50%. `colocated` does (−59%), but it is not in H6's list.
* **What it means:** the *loss* is robust. It is not created by pessimistic fills, and optimistic fills or latency would not rescue it. Even colocated (10/20 ms) loses: −1.69 / −3.45.
* **Caveat:** 4 of the 8 H6-variant runs were killed, so their magnitudes are censored, and higher_latency OOS is distorted by N1.
* Note that higher latency *raises* IS fills (829 vs 802). Slower requoting leaves quotes in the path of the mid. That supports the "fills come from the mid walking into us" reading (F6).

**Other hypotheses**
* **H4 holds.** In-sample gross is about +2.1 USDT on 26,035 notional, so the break-even maker fee is about 0.8 bp, against 2 bp paid.
* **H5 holds.** The 10 s markout per ETH CI is −0.198 [−0.287, −0.106] IS. The CI is optimistic because fills cluster.
* **H9 holds.** Funding is 1.3% of the IS net.
* **H7 untested.** The report has no hourly σ profile and no PnL by σ tercile.
* **H1:** identity gap 3e-11, 0 risk rejects. Quote recompute from logs was still not done.

## 4. Code review of the new pieces

**total_qty replace: correct** [CONFIRMED]
* `exchange.py:296-303` builds total = new remaining + old filled. `_place` re-adds `old.qty − old.remaining` (line 251), so the new order's qty equals `total_qty`.
* The strategy's `_filled` is kept per client id and cleared on every terminal state (`glft.py:459-464, 481-484`) and on gap (line 547).
* Exposure invariant: q_seen + r* ≤ Q holds. N4 lists the residual edge cases.

**Rate guards and `_Rolling`: correct** [CONFIRMED]
* The window drops items older than `now − window`. The empty window resets to exactly 0 (`glft.py:278-285`).
* Counts are integers, so there is no drift.
* Flatten orders skip the budget check but are counted (`glft.py:618-620`).
* Cancels are never blocked.
* `rate_throttled` = 0 and `modify_cap_cancels` = 0 in all 52 runs.
* `rate_throttled` counts blocked *cycles*, not orders. Fine, but say so in the summary docs.

**unknown_order grace: correct** [CONFIRMED]
* In the backtest, the report latency is constant (240 ms), so the fill report always precedes the race reject. Every case took the "explained" branch.
* The pending/expire path is covered by unit tests only. It matters for paper/live, where report ordering is not guaranteed.

**`--workers`: correct** [CONFIRMED]
* Jobs are pure functions of (config, period, seed).
* No module-level mutable state: `new_id` is per instance, there are no caches, and `deep_merge` deep-copies.
* Results are reassembled in config order (`run.py:152-162`).
* Bit-exact reproduction confirmed.
* Gaps:
  * no test covers parallel = sequential;
  * one failed job aborts the whole run (acceptable).
* *Fix (backtester):* add a tiny 2-worker vs 1-worker equality test on the synthetic fixture.

**IOC: correct.** See F1.
