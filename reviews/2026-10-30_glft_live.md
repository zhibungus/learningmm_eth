## 1. Warm start: causality and correctness [CONFIRMED]

**Nothing from the warm-up reaches the strategy, the account or the metrics**
* `engine.py` `warm_up` feeds only the exchange book and market view (no orders exist, so there can be no fills) and `MarketState._apply`. It skips the clock, the latency heap, the account and the recorder, and it raises if called after the start.
* Probe state at the first in-window event, both periods:
  * `account.cash` = 10000, `rec.first_ts` = None, heap = 0, `now` = 0.
  * Earliest strategy callback = window start (`on_start`), with **0 callbacks before `lo`**.
  * `rec.first_ts` = 00:00:00.001 (IS) and 00:00:00.000 (OOS, the archive funding print).

**The book is synced at window start**
* `warm_synced` = True, with bid/ask 2386.54 / .55 (IS) and 2357.64 / .65 (OOS).
* Last snapshots: 10-23 19:06:24.874 (IS) and 10-24 23:10:26.214 (OOS). These are exactly the `session_start` markers in `gaps/`.
* `check_warm_start` would have refused an unsynced book.

**The IS warm-up across the 10-23 recording is valid**
* The day files are partitioned by exchange `ts_ns`, not `recv_ts`. For example, the 10-24 depth file runs ts 00:00:00.072 → 23:59:59.994, while its recv times start on 10-23.
* The session from the 19:06:24 snapshot runs unbroken to the 15:10 disconnect on 10-24. I found **0 `pu ≠ previous u` breaks** in 70,387 depth updates within ±1 h of midnight, and there is no gap marker at 00:00.
* The OOS warm-up reads the last 50 min of the IS day. That is past data, used for book state only, so it is not leakage.

**`from_ns` does not change existing callers**
* `replay(from_ns=X)` equals `replay()` filtered to ts ≥ X, for 300,000 events that include the `session_start` gap and the snapshot.
* `from_ns=None` equals the old call.
* The change is just `lo = max(lo, from_ns)` (`loaders.py` `replay`).

**Minor, info only [CONFIRMED by reading]**
* `warm_up` applies warm-up quotes to the strategy's `MarketState` with no feed delay.
* This is harmless: the strategy acts only on in-window callbacks, and the first delivered in-window quote overwrites those prices first.

## 2. L2 fill realism

**Queue positions from the real book are plausible** [CONFIRMED]
* Over about 41k new orders and replaces per day, our quotes rest a median of **75 ticks** behind the touch (p10–p90: 52–96).
* We join behind a displayed queue of median **14 ETH** (p10 0.24, p90 about 60).
* Only **0.25–0.38% of placements land on an empty level** (front of queue). The 1,000-level REST snapshot (`config/data.yaml` `depth_limit`) plus full `@depth@100ms` diffs cover that depth.
* Queue fills began with a median 3–6 ETH ahead (p90 40–50). Only 1–2% started with nothing ahead.
* The median queue fill comes 1.3–1.8 s after (re)placement. These are sweeps that print at our level, not slow queue erosion.
* Trades are processed before the depth diff that reflects them (trade T ≤ depth T in the same 100 ms batch). `_observe` subtracts `traded_since_obs`, so trades are not double-counted as cancels (`fills.py:351-374`).

**Nothing in the queue model flatters PnL** [CONFIRMED]

| seed 42 | base (proportional) | cancel `all_behind` | queue factor 2 | `pessimistic_fills` (report: no queue fills at all) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IS net / queue fills | −2.93 / 81 | −2.80 / 45 | n/a | −2.90 / 0 |
| OOS net / queue fills | −1.63 / 91 | −1.74 / 49 | −1.72 / 56 | −1.18 / 0 |

* Halving or removing queue fills moves net by about the seed sd (0.3) or improves it, because most queue fills are replaced by through fills of the same orders.
* Queue fills are adverse, not free money.

**Partial fills** are capped by print size (through) and by the queue left after the public queue ahead (at-price). Our 0.01 ETH against queues of many ETH makes our own-queue effects negligible.

**Cross fills** are 13 / 9 per day and carry +0.01 USDT of capture. `no_cross_fills` gives −2.84 / −1.54, which is noise.

## 3. Leakage [CONFIRMED]
* **Calibration** (`calibration.json`):
  * `in_sample` 2026-10-24 only, written 16:18 UTC.
  * The backtest was written at 17:36, after it.
  * The loader filters on exchange `ts_ns` inside [lo, hi) of 10-24 (`glft_calibrate.py` `load_period_arrays`).
  * The files are ts-partitioned, so no 10-25 event can enter.
* **OOS 10-25** is used only by the backtest.
* **Gap timestamps in the live trade-gap mask:** the masked steps are the 653 s network outage (22:59:32–23:10:26) and one id jump. The `ts_ns` used is exchange time.
* **My earlier v1_1 claim was wrong.** I called the 2024 "5 single-id gaps" real. Spec v1.2.1 shows they were raw-id skips that Binance never publishes. The aggregate `trade_id` rule is the correct one, and the 2024 A and γ returning to the pre-v1.1 values is right.

## 4. Interpretation

**Live vs 2024, seed-42 headline**

| | 2024-03-29 IS | live 10-24 IS | live 10-25 OOS |
|---|---|---|---|
| σ (USDT/√s) / fee c (ticks) / h (ticks) | 0.25 / 71 / 100 | 0.18 / 47 / 76 | (σ frozen) |
| fills / day | 802 | 289 | 274 |
| spread capture / inventory / fees | +2.21 / −0.07 / −5.21 | +0.82 / **−2.52** / −1.23 | +0.76 / **−1.27** / −1.13 |
| gross (before fees) | +2.14 | **−1.70** | **−0.51** |
| 10 s mid move after fill | −1.41 bp | −2.04 bp | −2.15 bp |

*What changed*
* ETH is cheaper (fee per ETH lower), and live σ is lower, so h is about 76 ticks against 100.
* Quotes sit closer, and fills are more adversely selected per fill.
* In-sample, the loss is now driven by adverse selection: inventory −2.52 against fees −1.23.
* OOS, fees and gross are similar in size.
* **Gross is negative on both days.** That is a stronger negative than 2024 (H4 holds, and break-even maker fee < 0).

*Hypotheses*
* **H2: FALSIFIED, harder than 2024.**
  * Realised / Σ A·e^(−kδ_live)·dt = **0.156 IS / 0.138 OOS** (through-only: 0.105 / 0.088). The backtester's ≈ 0.17 agrees.
  * The model's fills are 6–7× too many. The re-pegging calibration measures something a persisting quote does not get (v1_1 F2).
  * The k CI 3.24–6.29 and A's grid dependence (0.33 / 0.19 / 0.15 at 100 / 500 / 1000 ms) say the same.
* **H3: HOLDS** on its metric.

  | | base | skew_off |
  |---|---|---|
  | mean \|q\| IS | 0.014 | 0.033 |
  | mean \|q\| OOS | 0.019 | 0.039 |
  | time at limit IS | 0.1% | 38% |
  | time at limit OOS | 2.9% | 52% |

  * skew_off PnL is mixed: IS −4.11 with **3/5 killed** (censored), OOS −0.69 with 156 fills against 276. That is "trade less" again, not evidence against the skew.
* **H8: HOLDS.** fee_shift has worse inventory control: \|q\| 0.024 / 0.032, time at limit 14% / 26%.
* **H5: HOLDS.**
  * The 10 s markout CI is below 0 on both days (−0.166 [−0.265, −0.059] and −0.186 [−0.320, −0.056] per ETH).
  * The 60 s IS CI includes 0.
* **H6: HOLDS for its list.**
  * pessimistic_fills, no_cross_fills, latency_spikes and higher_latency are all negative, and none cuts fills by more than 50% (pessimistic_fills −10%).
  * **Latency is the dominant lever:**
    * higher_latency: −4.56 / −3.36;
    * colocated: −2.25 / **+0.16**, with fills −72%.
  * The colocated sign flip is one seed, inside one seed-sd (0.26) of zero, and it is the optimistic assumption, so it is not evidence of an edge. It does say that stale quotes being picked off is the loss mechanism.
  * pessimistic_fills OOS −1.18, better than base −1.71, is the same signal: at-price fills are adverse.
* **H4: HOLDS** (fee-negative, and here also gross-negative).

**What two weekend days can tell us**
* 10-24 is a Saturday and 10-25 a Sunday: one IS day and one OOS day.
* Hourly σ ranges 0.07–0.57, with the 14:00–16:00 spike on 10-24.
* The seed sd (≈ 0.3) measures **latency-path noise only**. It says nothing about day-to-day market variation, which with n = 1 per period cannot be estimated.
* IS −2.9 vs OOS −1.7 is well within plausible day-to-day spread.
* Bucket t-stats are −2.0 / −1.2 and autocorrelated.
* **Supported:** the sign (negative), the mechanism (adverse selection plus latency), and the inventory hypotheses (H3/H8, large effects).
* **Not supported:** any PnL ranking of variants, any "colocated is profitable", or any IS→OOS stability claim.
* Weekday recordings are needed before v2 decisions.
